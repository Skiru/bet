## The two that were worth taking

### Napoli 1–2 Como — SUPERBETS @ 2.75 (boosted from 2.45) — WON

Legs: match shots on target > 6.5 · match goals > 1.5 · match corners > 7.5.
Landed 8 / 3 / 9.

Serie A joint rate for those three together: **38.6%** (27 of 70) **→ fair
2.59**. Offered 2.75. **Edge +2.2pp, expectation +6.1%.** The only slip of the
twenty that paid more than it was worth.

Why it worked as a *structure*: all three legs are high-probability volume
markets (73%, 78%, 71% league-wide) that move together — the goals↔SOT
correlation is +0.55, so the joint rate beats the 41.1% product of the
marginals. Positive correlation is a friend when you are on the *over* side of
all of it.

### Osasuna 1–0 Getafe — Getafe fouls over 12.5 @ 1.73 — WON (14)

No consensus line for team fouls, so this is priced off a sample. Getafe's last
eight away matches: 20, 24, 22, 16, 13, 13, 13, 17 fouls. **8/8, mean 17.2,
Wilson 95% lower bound 67.6% → minimum acceptable 1.48.** Offered 1.73.

League baseline for an away side clearing 12.5 fouls is 45%. Getafe are an
extreme, and the sample is unanimous. This is what a per-team counting line has
to look like before a short price is defensible.

Legs: 1H over 0.5 ✓ · 1H under 2.5 ✗ · 2H over 0.5 ✓. Half-time was 0–3; the
slip died at 25 minutes (goals 4′, 23′, 25′).

Legs one and two are the same variable in opposite directions: this is "the

likely one, but the bet was refusable before that was known.

## Tennis

Both legs won — Kalinskaya/Blinkova match aces over 3.5 @ 1.66, and Hurkacz over
18.5 games @ 1.44 in a 3–1 win. Neither can be priced: `bzzoiro-tennis` answers
`addon_required` (Sports Addon, $5/mo), which matches the standing note in
`provider-entitlement-faults`.

One structural observation worth carrying anyway, since it needs no data: **a
player's own game count in a best-of-five is a bet against dominance.** A 6-4
6-4 6-4 win gives exactly 18 games and loses an over-18.5. The bet needs a
fourth set, or a 7-5/7-6 somewhere. Backing the favourite's game total is
backing the underdog to compete, which is the opposite of how it reads.
